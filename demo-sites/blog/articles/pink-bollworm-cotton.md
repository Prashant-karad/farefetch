Pink bollworm larvae feed inside cotton bolls, so damage is often noticed only when bolls are opened. Pheromone traps are the best early warning: check them weekly and act when moth catches cross the recommended threshold. Destroy crop residue after the final picking and avoid extending the season with late irrigation, because a long season lets the pest build up. Rosette flowers, which look like twisted unopened buds, are an early sign of infestation and should be removed. Coordinated action across a whole village works far better than individual farms acting alone, since moths move between fields.
